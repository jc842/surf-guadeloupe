# Guadeloupe Surf Seasons & Swell Forecast: When to Score the Best Waves

Surfing in Guadeloupe is defined by two primary oceanic engines: North Atlantic winter depressions and tropical low-pressure systems. Understanding these meteorological seasons is the key to timing your Caribbean surf trip.

---

## The Prime Winter Season (November to April)
During the Northern Hemisphere winter, powerful low-pressure systems churn off Cape Hatteras and Greenland, sending long-period North and North-East groundswells across the Atlantic directly onto Grande-Terre.
- **Wave Heights**: 1.5m to 3.5m+ (5 to 12 ft)
- **Primary Spots**: Le Moule, Anse Bertrand, Port-Louis, Petit-Havre
- **Wind Pattern**: Steady Easterly Alizés (trade winds), usually cross-shore to offshore in early mornings.

## The Tropical & Hurricane Season (July to October)
Late summer brings tropical waves and hurricanes migrating across the Atlantic. While wind conditions can be unpredictable, hurricane groundswells produce some of the most legendary, heavy barreling sessions of the year.
- **Wave Heights**: Highly variable (0.5m flat days to 4m+ epic swells)
- **Top Picks**: Caribbean side reefs and south-facing points that rarely break during winter.

## Water Temperatures & Packing Checklist
The Atlantic and Caribbean waters around Guadeloupe remain between **26°C and 29°C (79°F to 84°F)** year-round.
- **Wetsuit**: None needed! A 1mm neoprene top or high-UV rashguard is sufficient for sun protection and board rub.
- **Wax**: Tropical wax only (warm/tropical water formula).
- **Reef Booties**: Essential for Le Moule and shallow coral ledges.
